# Bookkeeping Starter Kit

A clean, practical starter kit for a small LLC — a ready-to-use chart of accounts, a monthly routine you can actually stick to, and a plain-English guide to records and red flags.

Built for founders who are not accountants. Especially useful for single-member and small multi-member LLCs, including non-resident founders running a US LLC from abroad.

> General guidance only — not tax, legal, or accounting advice. Check your specific situation with a qualified professional.

## What's in this kit

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `chart-of-accounts.csv` | A starter chart of accounts (~43 accounts) you can import into QuickBooks, Xero, Wave, Zoho Books, or a spreadsheet |
| `monthly-routine.md` | The monthly close checklist, a weekly 15-minute habit, and a quarterly review |
| `records-and-red-flags.md` | What records to keep, how long to keep them, how to store them, and the red flags that cause trouble at tax time |

## How to use the kit

1. **Start with a separate business bank account** — see below. Nothing else in this kit works properly without it.
2. **Import the chart of accounts.** Open `chart-of-accounts.csv` in your accounting tool's import screen, or copy it into a spreadsheet if you're starting on spreadsheets. Delete accounts you'll never use, rename accounts to match your business, and don't add new accounts for every vendor — categories should stay broad.
3. **Connect / record consistently.** Link your business bank account and credit card if your tool supports it, or enter transactions weekly. Every transaction gets one account from the chart — no free-text categories.
4. **Run the monthly routine.** Follow `monthly-routine.md` in the first week of the following month. That's your monthly close.
5. **Keep records as you go.** Follow `records-and-red-flags.md` for receipts and storage, so tax season is a hand-off, not a rescue mission.

You don't need perfect bookkeeping on day one. You need consistent bookkeeping from day one.

## Why a separate business account matters

Never mix personal and business money. This is the single most important bookkeeping rule for an LLC.

- **It protects the LLC.** Commingling — paying personal expenses from the business account and business expenses from a personal account — is one of the classic reasons courts and auditors treat the business and the owner as the same thing. A clean separation helps show the LLC is a real, separate business.
- **It makes bookkeeping 10x easier.** If every transaction in the account is business, categorising takes minutes. If half the feed is personal, every month becomes detective work.
- **It makes taxes defensible.** At filing time you (or your preparer) need to prove income and expenses. A dedicated account, plus receipts, is that proof.
- **It makes banking and processors happy.** Payment processors, banks, and marketplaces expect business activity in a business account, in the business name / EIN where required.
- **It clarifies owner money.** Money you put in is an **Owner Contribution** (account 3000). Money you take out is an **Owner Draw** (account 3010). Draws are not salary and not a business expense — recording them correctly avoids a very common error.

Practical setup:

- One business checking account for operating money
- One business savings account for tax reserves (move a set percentage of every payout there)
- One business credit card, used only for business
- Payment processors (Stripe, PayPal, etc.) paying out only to the business checking account

## The monthly close concept

A "close" just means: once a month, you finish last month completely so it never has to be reopened.

A closed month means:

- Every bank and card transaction is entered and categorised
- Every account is reconciled to its statement (your balance matches the bank's)
- Receipts are matched to the expenses they prove
- The Profit & Loss is reviewed and makes sense
- Tax money is set aside, not spent

If you close every month, year-end is just the twelfth close. If you don't, year-end is twelve months of archaeology — slower, more expensive, and more error-prone. See `monthly-routine.md` for the step-by-step checklist.

## A note for non-resident founders

A US LLC owned by a non-resident still files US federal returns every year — even at $0 income — and still benefits from clean books from day one. Good records make Form 1120 / 5472, Form 1065, or your Schedule C / 1040-NR preparation dramatically simpler and cheaper. Keep the routine in this kit and hand your preparer a closed set of books, not a bank feed and a hope.

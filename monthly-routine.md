# Monthly Bookkeeping Routine

A repeatable routine beats heroic catch-ups. Do the weekly habit, close the month in the first week of the next month, and review properly once a quarter.

> General guidance only — not tax, legal, or accounting advice.

## The weekly 15-minute habit

Pick one fixed slot (e.g. Friday, 15 minutes) and do only this:

- [ ] Check the business bank and card feeds — nothing unfamiliar, nothing personal by mistake
- [ ] Categorise any new transactions while you still remember what they were
- [ ] Snap / save receipts for the week's purchases into your records folder (see `records-and-red-flags.md`)
- [ ] Chase any overdue customer invoices
- [ ] Move your tax set-aside to business savings if a payout landed (see below)

Fifteen honest minutes a week means the monthly close takes under an hour.

## The monthly close checklist

Do this in the first 7 days of the following month, for the month just ended.

### 1. Gather

- [ ] Download / confirm statements for: business checking, business savings, business credit card(s), Stripe / PayPal / other processors
- [ ] Collect all invoices issued, bills received, and receipts for the month
- [ ] Note any cash, owner-paid, or out-of-pocket business expenses separately — then reimburse / record them properly, don't leave them invisible

### 2. Reconcile accounts

- [ ] Reconcile business checking to its statement — ending balance matches
- [ ] Reconcile business savings to its statement
- [ ] Reconcile each business credit card to its statement
- [ ] Reconcile payment processors: payouts in the bank match processor reports, after fees
- [ ] Investigate any difference now, not at year-end — duplicates, missing entries, and bank errors are easiest to fix within a month

### 3. Categorise and clean up

- [ ] Categorise every uncategorised transaction — `6990 Uncategorized / Ask My Accountant` must be zero at close
- [ ] Split any mixed transactions (e.g. one payment covering software + a personal item) — record only the business part as business
- [ ] Record owner money correctly: money in = Owner Contributions, money out = Owner Draws (never income, never an expense)
- [ ] Record loan payments split between principal (Loans Payable) and interest (expense), if you have loans

### 4. Match receipts

- [ ] Match a receipt / invoice to every expense over your internal threshold (a common practice is every expense, and at minimum anything $75+)
- [ ] For meals and travel, add the business purpose and who was there, on the receipt or in the transaction note
- [ ] File unmatched items and chase missing receipts this week, while vendors can still resend them

### 5. Invoicing and receivables

- [ ] Confirm every job / sale from the month has been invoiced
- [ ] Review Accounts Receivable — who owes you, how old is it, who needs a nudge
- [ ] Write off / void anything genuinely uncollectible, with a note

### 6. Review the Profit & Loss

- [ ] Run the Profit & Loss for the month and compare it to the prior month and the same month last year (once you have one)
- [ ] Ask three questions: Is revenue complete? Does any expense category look unusually high or low? Is anything in the wrong account?
- [ ] Run the Balance Sheet too — bank balances agree to reconciliations, credit card and sales tax liabilities look real

### 7. Set aside tax money

- [ ] Move a set percentage of the month's profit / receipts to business savings as a tax reserve — pick a percentage with your tax professional and apply it consistently
- [ ] Note upcoming payments: estimated taxes, state annual report / renewal, registered agent renewal, sales tax filing
- [ ] If sales tax applies to you: confirm the month's Sales Tax Payable matches your sales records and calendar the filing

### 8. File the month's documents

- [ ] Save the month's statements, processor reports, invoices, bills, and receipts in your digital folder for that month (e.g. `2026 / 2026-11 /`)
- [ ] Export / back up your accounting file or reports (P&L + Balance Sheet as PDF)
- [ ] Lock / close the period in your accounting tool if it supports it, so closed months don't drift

## Quarterly review (every 3 months)

- [ ] Review P&L for the quarter vs. budget / prior quarter — pricing, margins, and the top 5 expense categories
- [ ] Review estimated tax position with your tax professional — adjust set-asides if income changed materially
- [ ] Check state obligations: annual report / renewal dates, sales tax filing frequency, any notices received
- [ ] Review subscriptions and recurring charges — cancel what the business no longer uses
- [ ] Check contractor files: W-9s (US contractors) / W-8BEN or W-8BEN-E (foreign contractors) collected before year-end 1099 / reporting season
- [ ] Clean up old receivables and payables — nothing should age forever without an explanation
- [ ] Back up the full records set to a second location (cloud + local, or two cloud services)

## Year-end handoff (bonus)

If you've closed every month, year-end is simple:

- [ ] Complete the December close
- [ ] Provide your preparer: P&L, Balance Sheet, bank / card / processor year-end statements, and your records folder
- [ ] List owner contributions and draws for the year
- [ ] Flag anything unusual in a short note — new lines of business, large purchases, loans, state changes

Clean books = a cheaper, faster, calmer filing.
